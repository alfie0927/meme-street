**What this says**

1. **Hardly anyone arrives, and reels are the only way in.** All reels together get about 110 views a day. If a good 1 to 2% of viewers visit, that is one or two visits a day. Nothing counts visits, so we cannot see where people drop out.
2. **The few who arrive meet a wall.** The reel says "Join free at memestreet.net". The home page then asks for a name, a password twice, an email, a date of birth and a 6-digit code from the inbox. New accounts stopped on 31 October, the day that code became compulsory, and no address has ever been confirmed on the live site. It may be coincidence (reels began on 30 October), but the code step may never have worked for a real visitor, least of all inside Instagram's own browser, where leaving to open the mail app can lose the form. Meanwhile the best "try it" page, the Daily Chart (two minutes, no account), is not where reels point. On the home page it is a small link in the header; the intro box offers only "Create an account" and "Sign in".
3. **Instagram shows the reels to almost no one, and the openings do not hold.** 31 views a reel is about a twentieth of what small accounts get. The best reels open with a challenge or an odd fact ("3 charts. Can you call what happens next?", 138 views; "This company doesn't use trucks.", 45.5% skipped). The worst open with a definition (85 to 89% skipped). Views fell from 113 a reel (the first five) to 35 (the next thirteen) while more were posted. Two carousels show about 100 likes on a reach of 4 to 6, so those likes did not come from people who saw the post. If followers or likes were ever bought, swapped or carried over, Instagram tests each reel on accounts that never watch, and the reel stops there.

**The sums** (my assumptions; nothing is measured yet): ten new accounts a day, at 5 in 100 visitors, needs about 200 visits a day. From reels alone at 1 to 2% that is 10,000 to 20,000 views a day, against about 110 now. Reels cannot carry this alone any time soon.

**So the order is:** find the leak, shorten the path from a link to a first game, fix the reels, add ways in that do not depend on Instagram, then give players reasons to return and bring friends. That is the marketing-plan skill's rule for this stage: "Fix the leak before pouring water in", with no paid ads until the path converts. Scored with that skill's checklist (my scoring), Meme Street gets roughly 9 to 15 out of 85: a strong product with almost nothing around it yet.

## The plan on one page

1. **The Daily Chart becomes the front door.** Every link (reel, bio, Story, post, directory) lands on today's chart, which needs no account. The account is offered after the result ("save your score and your streak"), takes under a minute and no longer waits for an emailed code.
2. **Fewer, better reels.** One a day, in the formats that already hold viewers, the question on screen and spoken in the first second, a daily "Daily Chart" series, and links people can tap (bio, Story sticker, a personal reply to every comment).
3. **The first 100 players by hand.** A "beat my score" link, daily-game directories, the same videos on TikTok, Shorts and Facebook, communities, investing societies and teachers, small creators, search.
4. **Underneath: counting.** The web server's visit log is switched on and every link carries a source tag, so each source is judged by finished games and new accounts, not by views.
