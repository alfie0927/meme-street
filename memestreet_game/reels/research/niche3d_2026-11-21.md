# The 3D story niche, studied: what the "In This Town" stories do (21 November 2026)

The owner: "watch through thousands of reels from my niche ... remake the entire structure of my reels and how they flow etc, to
get 100k views per reel on average." This is that study and what it changed. The earlier one (`tools/memestreet_daily_reel/
research/niche_2026-11-17.md`) covered flat money and trading Shorts; this one covers 3D animated stories.

## The short version

- The owner's exemplar (the Roblox story in `exemplar/10-42-35.mp4`) is **craftee_films' "In This Town, You Get 1,000,000
  Steps"** on Instagram: 749,000 likes. It belongs to a trend with a name, the **"In This Town / In This World" Roblox story**.
- The genre has one form, and its most-watched stories keep to it more strictly than its least-watched. The form is below and
  is now the brief of a new daily reel (`src/town_story.py`).
- **Nobody can promise 100,000 views a reel.** Small channels that copy this form on YouTube get a median of about 2,000 views
  a story (62 stories with the trend's words in their titles; 8% of them pass 100,000). The form is necessary, not sufficient: the numbers come to the studios that
  also have the audience (VoidCast 454,000 subscribers, Kalemod 257,000, craftee on Instagram). Our own town reels sit at
  about 1,200 views; our flat reels at about 120. The honest aim of this change is to move the town reels from 1,200 towards
  the 10,000 to 60,000 that the better small channels reach, and to measure it.

## What was done

- **Listed and ranked, by numbers only:** about 6,300 Shorts (two listings; a few are in both).
  - 87 searches across 3D animated stories (Roblox, Minecraft, low-poly, money and stock stories), then the newest 40 Shorts of
    the 114 channels behind them, each ranked against its own channel's median: 4,027 Shorts (`niche3d.json`). 1,766 had
    100,000 views or more, but they are almost all fandom animation (Roblox and Minecraft memes, songs, brand channels), not
    narrated stories.
  - 40 searches inside the trend itself, then its channels: 2,278 Shorts from 64 channels (`niche_town.json`).
- **Read, word for word:** 114 story scripts, cut out of the captions of four compilations (`stories.json`), 60 of them
  VoidCast's whole October output, **published by the studio in order of views** ("ranked from most watched to least"): the one
  list in the study where the creator, the look and the audience are the same and only the story differs.
- **Watched, frame by frame:** 20 stories of the trend's channels (twelve frames each, cuts counted, captions read), from
  331,000 views down to 953 (two of them from channels whose median is under 1,000), and our own town reels the same way.
- **The exemplar's own page:** its top comments are loopholes in the rule: "Bro just leave the town" (171,365 likes), "Just
  get a wheelchair" (87,570), "Town full of calisthenics" (19,344).
- Instagram itself cannot be listed without the owner's logged-in browser, and TikTok gave only craftee's 14 videos (a median
  of 1,037 views there: the account lives on Instagram).

## The form (what a story is made of)

A median story is **128 seconds and 556 words at 4.4 words a second**, in sentences of 7 to 8 words. In order:

1. **The rule, as the first words:** "In this town, running turns you gold." "In this town, hugs run out." Four to eight
   words, one physical rule that a body or a thing visibly obeys.
2. **"You see, ..."**: how it works, with an exact number and a sound word ("Clink. 10 seconds later, you're a statue."), and
   one thing "everyone says" about the rule ("Little kids never feel it back. That's what everyone says.").
3. **"So, everybody here ..."**: three or four gags of how the town copes, with named neighbours ("Mr. Pruitt ran from a bee.
   He's been at the bus stop since Tuesday."), one of them silly.
4. **"Me? ..."**: the narrator's odd place in the rule ("I'm 12, and I still have all 100. I'm saving them for something big.").
5. **The one they love** (a little brother, a dad), a daily ritual with a number in it ("My record was 9 seconds"), and a line
   that will come back ("You'll thank her one day").
6. **The slip:** the rule bites once, small.
7. **The crisis:** the loved one in danger because of the rule, the town unable to help because of the rule, a clock in
   numbers ("15 minutes left").
8. **The move:** the narrator uses the rule itself as the tool; it nearly fails.
9. **The twist:** what everyone said was wrong ("Running never turned anyone gold. Running away did.").
10. **The callback:** the ritual returns turned round ("Now Benji runs home to me. His record is 12 seconds.").

No sign-off. The studio's own line, when there is one, is a joke in the story's terms.

## What separates the most-watched from the least-watched (VoidCast's 60, top 20 against bottom 20)

| | Top 20 | Bottom 20 |
|---|---|---|
| Opens "In this town" (not "In this world") | 94% | 50% |
| "You see," in the opening | 88% | 50% |
| Sound words per 100 words | 0.93 | 0.55 |
| Named neighbours per 100 words | 0.91 | 0.36 |
| Length | 139 s | 133 s |
| Words a second | 4.5 | 4.7 |

The top of the list is rules about family, the body and feelings (you feel your sibling's pain, fear turns you to stone, running
turns you gold, 100 hugs, waiting turns you into a tree). The bottom is "X is deadly" and "it takes you" (being late, stepping
on cracks, sneezing, umbrellas, the night bus), and "everyone has a health bar" is 52nd of 60. Length and pace do not separate
them.

## What the picture does (the 20 watched)

- The stories at 20,000 views or more: 112 seconds, **6.2 cuts every 10 seconds** (a new shot every 1.6 s), 4.2 words a second.
- Full-frame 3D blocky people in close and mid shots; the rule is **on screen as a number or a badge from frame 0** (a health
  bar over a head, "5 FLOORS" on a house, a number on a chest) and it changes on the word, with a colour flash.
- Captions of one to three words, low in the frame, white with a black outline, the key word in colour.
- The end: a question on screen and "COMMENT BELOW" ("What word heals YOU?").
- The flops: a flat stick-figure cartoon in a small letterboxed frame, told in six parts (a median of 35 views); a 25-second
  cut with SUBSCRIBE buttons over it (1,300). The flat stick-figure look is the genre's losing look, as it is on our account.

The town reels already hold strangers past the hook. What they lack is what carries a reel past its second batch of viewers:
people finishing it, commenting and sending it. The serial's parts stop on a cliffhanger and a lesson; the genre's stories
resolve, and their comment sections are arguments about the rule.

## What changed

- **A new daily reel, "In this town"** (`src/town_story.py`, the 13:00 slot): one complete story on the ten beats, 330 to 420
  words (about 95 seconds: the genre's shorter half, because a 3D second costs about a minute of render here), opening "In this
  town", "You see," next, named neighbours, sound words, family, nothing deadly, one loophole left open for the comments, no
  sign-off. The rule makes money, prices, luck or risk physical; the twist carries one trading idea as a truth about people
  (twelve ideas, taken in turn), never as a trading word. The checks are the form's own (`errors()`), with the length and
  short sentences of a remake (`form` in the brief).
- **The closing question on screen** (`"ask"`, with COMMENT BELOW over the last four seconds, `reel.html`), and a caption whose
  first line is the rule and a question that invites a loophole.
- **Every town reel at 24 frames a second** (a fifth less to render), and a longer limit for a render.
- **18:00** takes turns between the crazy stock history and the explainer, both in the town.

## What to watch next (the measure of whether this worked)

`state/reel_ledger.json` tells the looks apart by type ("In this town", "Blocky story", "<format> 3D"). After ten town stories:
their median views against the serial's 1,160; watched against 30 to 46%; comments and sends against zero. If the stories do
not beat the serial's parts, the form is not the lever on this account and the next thing to test is the voice (the genre's is
a lively child's voice; ours is Kokoro) and the look (their sets are richer).

## Limits of this study

YouTube stood in for Instagram. View counts on YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels are not the same currency. The 60-story
ranking is one studio's month. The frames of 20 videos were read by eye.
