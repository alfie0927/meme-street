# How the winning faceless Shorts are made (the production slice, 22 November 2026)

The owner, 22 November: "we will COPY the winning production style exactly, with no differences, and use that ONE style for every
reel." This file is the production half of the reel library: which ways of making a faceless story or explainer Short exist in money,
business and history, which of them win, and for the three best an exact COPY SPEC measured from their own frames and sound.
The numbers are in `production.json` beside this file; the frame sheets are in `D:/reel-tmp/lib_prod/sheets` (not in the repo).

## The short version

- **One way of making a Short wins far more reliably than the others: a 3D picture of the thing itself happening, with a camera
  that never stops.** Zack D. Films makes nothing else, and it is the only channel in either listing in which every listed Short
  is over a million views (40 of 40; the lowest has 1,400,000; median 5,050,000). Terra Mystica, which makes the same kind of
  picture, has 36 of 40 over a million (median 4,000,000).
- **The second is flat 2D animation of Kurzgesagt's kind** (median 1,300,000; 30 of its newest 40 over a million). Extra History and
  Knowledgia, which also draw every frame, sit at the same level (1,350,000 and 1,800,000).
- **The form the business-story channels themselves use, a narrator over stock and news footage, is the weakest of the three**:
  medians of 15,500 to 181,000 with a few Shorts at 1 to 27 million. It is also the one we cannot make: it needs other people's
  footage.
- **The three winners agree on almost everything that is not the picture**, and our reels differ from all three on the same points.
  A voice that starts on frame 0 and talks at 3.4 to 3.75 words a second, with about half a second or more at each full stop in
  six of the seven; one small caption line low in the frame that swaps whole, with no pop and no word-by-word highlight; no logo,
  no header, no title card held, no end card; no drum beat under the voice. That list is the cheapest thing in this file to copy
  and it is the same whichever picture is chosen.
- **What cannot be promised.** A channel's median is its audience as much as its style, and subscriber counts were not read.
  This slice shows which production styles the big, steady channels use. It cannot show that the style made them big.

A channel is filed under the style of the one or two of its Shorts that were watched. The medians are over each channel's newest
40 or so Shorts.

- **A. 3D scene, the thing itself shown.** A 3D animation of the event or the mechanism: a woman falls from a window and hangs
  from a rack; a watermelon is cut with dental floss; a man buys five bottles of milk. At its best it is one continuous shot and
  the camera never stops moving (Zack D. Films). MagnatesMedia's "Funny Pricing Tactic" is the same idea with low-poly people who
  have no faces, and it is that channel's second biggest Short at 29 times its median. The Infographics Show's biggest is a 3D
  medical animation (10 times its median). Terra Mystica ("If you raise the Paraceratherium, it will see you as its parent") is
  the same picture with 4 cuts in 32 s; its woman changes face between shots, so it may be made with a video model and not
  rendered. neo opens on a few seconds of real footage and then stays on a 3D globe. greenscreencut's silent 3D card comparing
  gravity on each planet has 36,000,000.
- **B. Flat 2D animation.** Every sentence drawn: Kurzgesagt's flat vector people and yellow label plates, Extra History's
  cartoon heads, Knowledgia's painted map, Newsthink's single diagram, Casually Explained's stick figures on a white page. In
  Kurzgesagt's, a hard cut every 3 seconds, and between moves the picture holds still.
- **C. Footage documentary.** A narrator over stock, news and archive clips, a new shot every 1 to 2 seconds. How Money Works,
  Jake Tran and Logically Answered make these; so do the channels that re-upload a found clip with a voice over it (Mysterious
  Media, Archive Central) and those that mix in AI stills (Shubham Rajput, BRIGHT SIDE).
- **D. Post card over a clip.** Six to twelve seconds, a fake social post above a found clip, looping. Some of the biggest single
  counts in the listings (74,000,000; 61,000,000; 36,000,000) on medians of 1,400 to 256,500. YouTube counts a Short again each
  time it replays, so a six-second loop collects views a 40-second story cannot, and the clip is someone else's.
- **E. AI-generated video.** A made-up "found tape" history (mrstorytelling on TikTok, median 421,250), a time-lapse that never
  happened (Stone Reborn, 27,000,000 once, median 7,050).

## Which three, and in what order

1. **A, the 3D scene.** The highest and steadiest numbers, and the nearest to what we already have (the 3D town in `world3d.js`).
2. **B, flat 2D animation.** The next steadiest, and native to `reel.html`: a scene function that draws SVG.
3. **C, the footage documentary.** The form of our own niche, lower and far less steady, and the only one of the three we cannot
   make today.

D and E are not specified below: D's numbers are loop counts over borrowed clips, and E needs a paid video model the owner has
turned off.

## COPY SPEC A: the 3D scene in one shot (Zack D. Films)

Exemplars: [Cutting A Watermelon With Dental Floss](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/VOI9-Gx2EaY) (17,000,000),
[Woman Hangs Upside Down Seven Floors Up](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/3501sPb6ZmM) (14,000,000), and the low-poly variant,
MagnatesMedia's [Funny Pricing Tactic](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/A1zK48vHp3k) (2,000,000). Where the variant differs it has
its own line.

**Picture**
- 1080×1920, 30 frames a second.
- **Zero cuts.** 0 cuts in 35.7 s and 0 in 47.5 s. The one the counter found at 23.7 s is a camera whip of 0.97 s (sheet
  `3501sPb6ZmM_tr.jpg`); it also found 1 and 10 softer changes, which are fast camera moves as far as the sheets show. The story
  is one continuous move through one scene; things change in front of the camera.
  *Variant:* one scene as well. The counter's 3 cuts are all within 0.4 s of 8.0 s: a full-screen price card (green over red,
  "1 = $1" above "5 = $7" in white) flashing and fading back into the shop (`A1zK48vHp3k_tr.jpg`).
- **The camera never rests.** No frame is still in any of the three (0%). The mean change of a pixel from one frame to the next
  is 6.6 and 14.6 on a 0 to 255 scale (ours can be compared with the same measure). Only 4 of the 70 stretches measured are a
  clean push or pull, and those run at 14 to 20% a second; the rest are an orbit or a move with the subject and fit no zoom.
  *Variant:* a forward dolly through the shop that reads as 21% a second (9 of 11 clean stretches zoom in); its measure is 4.2.
- **Frame 0:** the subject in the middle of the frame in a mid shot, already doing the thing: the knife is in the watermelon; the
  woman stands at the open window with the bottle. The first two words are on the caption line ("Cut the", "She fell"). No title,
  no logo, no header. By 0.5 s the event has started (she is falling). Sheets `VOI9-Gx2EaY_io.jpg`, `3501sPb6ZmM_io.jpg`.
- **Look:** photoreal 3D, people with faces and moving hands, daylight, mid-key, the scene's own colours (no grade, no brand
  colour). Palettes in the table. *Variant:* flat-shaded low-poly people without faces, a dim teal-grey interior with warm lamps,
  strong depth-of-field blur on whatever is near the lens (`A1zK48vHp3k_io.jpg`).

## D, the post card over a clip, roughly

Not measured to be copied; read off `z-Oj1nao5xg_f0.jpg` (History Peek TV, 74,000,000, 7.0 s) and the two like it.

- A black 1080×1920 canvas. An avatar about 150 px across with the channel's name and a blue tick beside it, centred, at about
  y 110 to 260. Under it eight short centred lines of white text, about 44 px, with the key words in colour (a date and a place in
  green, the subject in amber, the danger in red), from about y 280 to y 680.
- The clip sits under the text from about y 690 to y 1600, full width, with black below it.
- 6 to 9 seconds, no narrator, a music clip at -7.5 to -18 LUFS, the text on screen from frame 0 to the end. It loops.

## Limits

- **The style is tangled with the audience.** Zack D. Films and Kurzgesagt had millions of subscribers before they made
  Shorts. Within one channel there is a single clean comparison: MagnatesMedia's low-poly 3D story at 29 times that channel's
  median. It is one video, and the channel's other Shorts were not watched.
- **Seven winners, no losers, were measured precisely.** The shared list is what winners do. Whether flops do it too is not known.
- **35 videos were watched**, chosen to cover the styles. Shares of the market by style cannot be read from them.
- **YouTube stood in for Instagram**, and a YouTube view is not an Instagram view.
- **Sound was measured and not heard.** Effects could not be told from music; "no beat" is a detector's answer.
- **Fonts** were matched against 84 candidates. Two matched well (Tahoma Bold 0.90, Montserrat Bold 0.81 to 0.87); Kurzgesagt's was
  not among them.
- **Views are the listing's rounded figures** (YouTube shows "17M"), read on 22 November 2026.
- The word times come from a small speech model on this PC and are good to about 0.05 s; it cannot place the very first word
  more exactly than "within the first 0.2 s".
