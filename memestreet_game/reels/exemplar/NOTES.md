# What the reference reels do (12 reels the owner saved, watched frame by frame on 2026-11-09)

These are references for the CRAFT. Never copy a device: invent your own for every reel. Thousands of reels like these
exist; these twelve only show the level.

## The shape they share
- ONE visual device carries the whole reel. It is literal to the lesson and it is set up in the first second:
  - a stick figure swings a hammer at a boulder marked BREAK-EVEN; a counter ticks "trades logged" 1 to 100; every
    swing is a trade (+1R, -1R, "revenge trade", "moved my stop"); on trade 101 the boulder cracks and light pours out:
    "it wasn't trade 101, it was the 100 you logged" (a journaling lesson).
  - gold is a lift car hanging from three cables; each cause (the Fed, oil, a tech crash) cuts one cable on its words
    and the car drops a floor; analysts' price targets are floors the lift might stop on; "which floor? comment".
  - a weather forecast board: "storm warning", "cold front", "lightning", "partly cloudy", each a weather icon with
    the market fact under it (the jobs number, the first test of 4,000, a -3.5% session); ends "storm or sunshine?
    comment your forecast".
  - two stick traders on diving boards over a pool: the demo trader never jumps ("too risky", "one more backtest";
    HUD: backtests 1,247 and climbing, live trades 0); the live trader jumps small, belly-flops, and gets better
    (HUD: live trades 1, 2, 6, 17, 90); "demo can't teach you what a real loss feels like".
  - two figures hammer a rock side by side: one "quits at 99", one "hits 100"; the counters run together to 99; one
    walks away; the other's next hit cracks it: "you never know which hit breaks it".
  - a sellers <-> buyers tug bar sits above each candle pattern, shifting as the candles form.
  - pure chart: candles draw one by one; a level, a zone, one glowing candle; a camera push onto that candle; little
    red SL tags sit under the equal lows and vanish when the sweep takes them.
- A HUD that changes under the story: a counter, an account line (sparkline) that climbs, a hike-odds bar, a date.
  It is small, top of frame, and the viewer watches it move.
- Punch captions: 3 to 5 words, one word in colour ("Your first 100 trades", "it broke the lows", "Still break-even").
  Never a full sentence. The voice says the full thought; the screen shows the punch.
- A twist or payoff near the end (the boulder cracks; the chart does the opposite of what it looked like), then one
  short ask (comment a word, save, follow), then the sign-off.
- Length 40 to 120 s. Something changes on screen every 2 to 3 seconds: a count ticks, a candle draws, a tag lands,
  a figure moves. Nothing sits still, nothing swims.
- Each reel owns its palette and mood: mono white on black; teal/red on near-black; gold on dark brown with hazard
  stripes; storm blue with rain; a soft blue night with a moon. Two or three colours, used with discipline.

## How they are built (the things to imitate)
- The hook frame already shows the device, the HUD and the first punch: no empty first second.
- Scale: the device fills the middle of the frame (y 520..1290). Figures are big (the hammer figure is a third of the
  frame tall). Text is big (punch 54-64 px, HUD 26-34 px, nothing smaller).
- Motion has weight: a swing winds up and follows through; a fall accelerates and lands with dust; a crack starts as a
  line, then the pieces fly; light bursts out of a crack in rays. Impacts shake the frame a few pixels.
- Repetition with change: the same swing 20 times, but the counter, the tag and the account line change every time,
  so repetition reads as progress.
- Sound-off test: a viewer with the sound off can follow the whole story from the device, the HUD and the punches.

## Where to find more (for your own ideas, never to copy)
Devices that work for money lessons: a scale that tips; a bucket with a hole (fees, overtrading); a staircase up and a
lift down (slow gains, fast losses); a race between a tortoise and a hare account; a thermometer; a fuse burning down to
an earnings date; a tug of war; a domino run; a seesaw; a leaky boat being bailed; a climber and a rope (the stop); a
slot machine against a chess clock; a garden where some seeds grow (diversification); a traffic light; a speedometer.
Pick the one that IS the lesson, then make it specific to this reel's facts.

## What the niche's biggest lesson videos do (42 watched frame by frame, 2026-11-15: swipe.md, "Teardowns")
Shapes to use, never words or drawings to copy. Winners beat their own channel's usual views at least fivefold; flops
are the same channels' videos under half of it.
- Frame 0 is ONE thing a stranger knows at a glance, and it fills 40% or more of the frame within a second (28 of 29
  winners, 1 of 6 flops). The flop's first frame is a blank stage, a blur or an unlabelled line.
- The promise is on screen as big, high-contrast words by 1.5 seconds (16 of 29 against 1 of 6).
- A lesson that travels gives a RULE the viewer can apply (an entry and a stop, an if/then, an exact setting) and plays a
  trade out to its RESULT on screen (the box fills; the target or the stop is hit). One that only explains what a thing
  is, with no rule and no outcome, is the flop. Same channel, same style: "one named setup, its rules, one trade to its
  end" won 56 times the usual views; "X explained" over a stock picture got a fifth of them.
- A test is the proof, never the headline: no video whose subject was "we tested it" was a big winner. The evidence comes
  late and takes seconds (a tally, a table, a curve). The picture that read best: N runs as N tiles that fill.
- With no chart to show: a fixed stage (two figures, or a headline that stays) and one new picture of your own per
  sentence in the same slot; a naive question stating a surprising fact, flipped by second 4; a number by second 8.
- The pace is one change per thing said, each change SHOWING it. Cutting faster did not help (the fastest-cut video
  flopped), nor did length, a number in the hook or a presenter.
- Seven winners end by running back into their opening shot or line; no flop does. Five seconds of outro card is a
  flop's ending.
