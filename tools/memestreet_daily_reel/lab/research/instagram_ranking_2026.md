# Instagram Reels in 2025-2026: what drives views, watch time, shares and follows

Research for @memestreetgame (448 followers, 19 posts, faceless animated-chart reels, synthetic voice-over, ~7 posts/day).
Compiled 2026-11-07. Every claim has a source URL and a confidence tag.

**Confidence key**
- **HIGH** = official Instagram/Meta page or statement, Meta developer docs, or a very large dataset.
- **MEDIUM** = reputable study, trade press quoting Instagram, or several agreeing sources.
- **LOW** = single blog, vendor claim or opinion, or something I could not trace to a primary source.

**How the evidence was gathered (read this before relying on a number)**
- Instagram Help Center pages and Meta Transparency Center "system cards" are JavaScript pages that my fetcher could not read. Where I cite them I say so, and the content comes from search-result excerpts of the Meta page or an article reproducing it (tagged MEDIUM at best).
- Meta developer docs were read through the markdown renderings (`.../documentation/....md`) of the same pages, fetched 2026-11-07.
- Instagram for Creators pages (`creators.instagram.com`) and Meta's earnings-call PDF were read directly. Where a quote from an Instagram or Meta page is in quotation marks I re-fetched the page to confirm the wording; quotes attributed to Mosseri come from trade press, not from his posts.
- Several vendor pages contradict themselves (Socialinsider's sample counts, Trial Reels follower thresholds). I note each case.
- Instagram does not publish ranking weights. Any "X times more important" claim is not Instagram's unless stated.

## 1. How Instagram ranks Reels now

### 1.1 Signals Instagram / Mosseri have actually named

- **[HIGH] Cold-start test on non-followers (Feb 26, 2025, Instagram for Creators).** "Among non-followers, your reel is initially recommended and shown to a small audience of people that we think will enjoy it." "The best-performing reels are then shown to a slightly wider audience." "Signals include user activity, such as watch time, retention, shares, likes and comments." Content is distributed "based on its engagement, not just the creator's follower count." "Recommendations make it more difficult to predict the reach of every post." Same post says: "Start with a compelling hook in the first three seconds", "post consistently", "your reels should make sense to those who may not know you", "Incorporating audio to reels can bring them to life and grab attention with new audiences." https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through
- **[HIGH] April 30, 2024 post** introducing the same system: eligible content "is shown to a small audience that we think will enjoy it" (whether or not they follow the poster), then "the top-performing set of reels are shown to a slightly wider audience"; "A new input to ranking that will give smaller creators more distribution" (the post does not say what it measures); the goal is to "give all creators a more equal chance of breaking through to new audiences." https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-Originality
- **[HIGH] "Instagram Ranking Explained" (Mosseri, May 31, 2023).** Reels is "designed to help you discover new things, with an emphasis on entertainment." Instagram surveys people: "we survey people and ask whether they find a particular reel worth their time or fun." Predicted actions include resharing, watching to the end, liking and visiting the audio page ("a proxy for whether or not you might be inspired to make your own reel"). Signal groups, in rough order: your activity, your history with the poster, information about the reel (audio, visuals, popularity), information about the poster (popularity, to "give everyone a chance to find their audience"). https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained
- **[MEDIUM] Mosseri, Jan 22, 2025 (quoted by Social Media Today).** "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." Creators should check "average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." No weights were given and comments were not mentioned. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/
- **[MEDIUM] Watch time counts both ways (Mosseri video, reported Feb 27, paraphrase).** Instagram considers "the percentage of a video watched and the total seconds viewed"; the example given is that 10 seconds on a 1-minute video holds the same weight as 10 seconds on a 10-second video. https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/instagram-reveals-how-its-algorithm-weighs-watch-time-for-reels
- **[MEDIUM] Sends per reach.** Mosseri raised it in an AMA at the end of May 2024, then in a Colin & Samir interview, then a Threads post in July 2024 calling it one of the most important ranking signals (paraphrase). https://lindseygamble.beehiiv.com/p/unpacking-instagram-s-focus-on-sends-per-reach. The widely repeated "sends are worth 3-5x likes" is a third-party estimate; I found no Instagram statement of any multiplier (a tracker also lists it as unverified: https://keywordseverywhere.com/news/instagram-algorithm-updates/, LOW).
- **[MEDIUM] Reels Chaining system card (Meta Transparency Center, updated Nov 11, 2025).** Listed prediction: "How likely you are to watch less than 3 seconds of a reel." Listed input: "How many times the post has been skipped within 2 seconds of being opened." Sound-on views are also an input. Search-result excerpts of the same page also mention a prediction about reusing a reel's audio and a pipeline where a lightweight model first cuts candidates to about 100 reels. One excerpt also described a length-normalised completion prediction (watching more than 95% of users who watched reels of the same length); the reproduction linked below instead places the "more than 95% of a video" prediction on the Explore card, not the Reels card, so treat that point as unresolved. Page: https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/ig-reels-chaining/ (unreadable by my fetcher); reproduction of the quoted lines: https://www.aisocialmedianow.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026. Meta says signals "change frequently over time" and gives no weights.
- **[HIGH] Instagram scale statements.** "Reels makes up over 50% of all time spent on Instagram." "Each part of the Instagram app uses its own algorithm." https://creators.instagram.com/how-to-grow

### 1.2 Connected vs unconnected (non-follower) reach for small accounts

- **[HIGH]** Official position: a new reel is tested on a small audience of non-followers chosen because Instagram thinks they will enjoy it, then expanded in stages (Feb 2025 and Apr 2024 posts above). Instagram gives no figure for the share of reach that comes from non-followers (the Feb 2025 post gives none).
- **[MEDIUM]** Mosseri's Sept 2026 ranking series named four principles beyond engagement: originality, recency, "breaking content" and larger AI models. On breaking content he said Instagram "will show content from small creators who post publicly to accounts that don't follow that creator", "with a view to boosting posts from new creators." Social Media Today, Sept 24, 2026: https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/ . Parasocial Magazine quotes his advice to track engagement rate "as opposed to total views or likes": "of all the people who saw the post, how many of them actually engaged with that post?" https://www.parasocialmagazine.com/news/mosseri-names-four-instagram-ranking-principles-for-creators/
- **[MEDIUM, inference from the statements above]** With 448 followers almost all early distribution is "unconnected", where sends are "slightly more important" than likes and the first audience is chosen by interest match. That makes the first 3 seconds, watch time and shares per reach the levers, not likes.
- **[LOW] Not confirmed:** one blog describes an "audition system"; I could not tie it to any Instagram document.

### 1.3 Quality gates Instagram states it applies before recommending

- **[HIGH] Format (Instagram for Creators, Aug 30, 2022, updated Apr 25, 2023):** use "high-resolution, 9x16 vertical videos with no borders, where text does not cover the majority of the screen"; "Don't post reels that contain watermarks, that are blurry or otherwise low quality"; "In order to be recommended, your content has to be 3 minutes or less"; "Pull the viewer in within the first 3 seconds, and keep them interested the whole time"; "Search results are matched by text, so make sure that you're including relevant keywords." Professional accounts can check eligibility at Settings > Account > Account Status. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators
- **[HIGH] Less likely to be recommended (May 2023 post):** "low-resolution or watermarked reels", "reels that are muted or contain borders", "reels that are majority text", "reels that have already been posted on Instagram." https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained
- **[MEDIUM] Recommendation Guidelines categories:** "sensitive or low-quality content about health or finance" (Facebook-era example: "misleading or deceptive business models (like payday loans)"), "content that users broadly dislike" (clickbait, engagement bait, contests and giveaways) and low-quality publishing (unoriginal or repurposed content). Such content is not removed, just "not shown in places where we recommend content." https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-provides-an-overview-of-content-that-it-looks-to-exclude-from-its/584442 ; Instagram's own page https://help.instagram.com/313829416281232 (unreadable by my fetcher; a search excerpt lists the same Health or Finance category).
- **[HIGH] Originality (Apr 30, 2026):** "75% of recommendations in the US now coming from original posts"; re-uploads with only "a border, watermark, subtitles or a credit in the captions" are unoriginal; accounts that mostly post unoriginal content are removed from recommendation surfaces and can regain eligibility on a rolling 30-day basis; followers still see their posts. Meme templates count as original if they are materially edited. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram and https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines/ . The account's own animated charts and scripts are original by this test.

## 2. The "skip rate" metric

- **[HIGH] Definition.** Meta API: `reels_skip_rate` = "The percentage of views from people who skipped during the first 3 seconds of the reel." Flagged "estimated" and "in development." https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media/insights.md . Instagram announced it (via @creators on Threads, reported Aug 24, 2025) as replacing "view rate": "skip rate is the percentage of views from people who decided to skip your reel during those first 3 seconds." It arrived in the API on Dec 3, 2025 (changelog link in section 4). https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/
- **[HIGH] Companion Retention chart.** Instagram: "With Retention, you'll always see a downward slope, but the flatter the line, the more engaged your audience is." The retention curve is in the app; the API gives only `ig_reels_avg_watch_time`, `ig_reels_video_view_total_time` and `reels_skip_rate`. (same SMT link)
- **[HIGH] What a good value is: Instagram publishes no benchmark.** Confirmed by Metricool (https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/) and an API write-up (https://adaptlypost.com/blog/reels-skip-rate, MEDIUM).
- **[HIGH, vendor data] Measured averages.** Socialinsider calculated skip rate ("the percentage of Reel plays in which viewers swiped away within the first 3 seconds") over 9,771,822 organic Reels from brand accounts with 1K-1M followers, Jan-Jun 2026: 65.50% (1-5K followers), 65.30% (5-10K), 63.40% (10-50K), 62.60% (50-100K), 60.50% (100K-1M). Bigger accounts skip less. Caveat: the page's methodology contradicts itself on account-size exclusions and its tier counts sum to 5 more than the stated total. https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/
- **[LOW] Popular "good skip rate" bands** ("below 20% strong, 20-30% healthy, above 40% a problem") are the publisher's own "targets", with no dataset behind them: https://retensis.com/blog/instagram-reels-skip-rate-benchmarks-2026 . They conflict with the measured ~60-65% average above, so do not use them as pass/fail lines.
- **[HIGH for Instagram's statements, LOW for vendor tactics] What Instagram says lowers it.** Nothing quantified. Statements: pull viewers in within the first 3 seconds (2022/2023 page and Feb 2025 post); reels must "make sense to those who may not know you"; add audio (Feb 2025 post). Meta's Reels card also treats a skip within 2 seconds as a model input (section 1.1, MEDIUM). Vendor tactics (one promise per reel, show the result first, text matching the spoken line) are advice only: https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/lower-instagram-reels-skip-rate (LOW).
- **Practical use:** compare each reel with your own trailing median; treat the ~60-65% brand average as an outside reference, not a target.
- **[MEDIUM] API caveats:** Reels only, lifetime period, data can lag up to 48 hours, a missing value comes back as an empty data set, not 0, and the unit (0-1 or percent) is not stated on Meta's page (https://adaptlypost.com/blog/reels-skip-rate, MEDIUM).

## 3. Trial Reels

### 3.1 How they work (official)
- **[HIGH] Instagram for Creators, Dec 10, 2024:** switch on "Trial" before sharing; trial reels are shown to non-followers first; only the creator can see that a reel is a trial; it stays off the profile grid until shared with everyone; followers will not see it in feed or the Reels tab, though it may appear in a DM someone shares or on a page for the same audio, location or filter. Graduation is manual ("share with everyone") or automatic: Instagram shares it with followers "if it performs well based on views in the first 72 hours." Insights show about 24 hours after sharing (views, likes, comments, shares) with comparisons to your previous trials. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels
- **[HIGH]** Instagram's Reels page: "You must have a professional account to use trial reels." https://creators.instagram.com/reels
- **[MEDIUM] Instagram's own numbers (June 8, 2025, Social Media Today quoting Instagram):** "After trying trial reels, 40% of creators started posting reels more often" and "80% saw an increase in reels reach from non-followers" (the 80% refers to the creators who posted more, not all users); limit of 20 trial Reels per day. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-trial-reels-increase-reach-tests/750121/ . I could not find the original Instagram post, and a search result describing the sample as more than 400,000 creators is unverified (LOW).
- **[MEDIUM] Mosseri on reach (quoted by Buffer):** "Because it's not going to your followers, you can't compare it to a normal reel", and trials are "going to get less reach, almost always" - so compare trials with trials. https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-trial-reels/ . Sept 2026: Mosseri recommended trial reels for experimenting because trends change quickly (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/).
- **[MEDIUM, conflicting] Eligibility is contradictory.** Planoly, quoting Instagram's Help Center: public account that is eligible for recommendations; professional accounts need at least 200 followers, personal accounts 1,000; "If your public account is ineligible to appear in recommendations, you will also lose access to trial reels." (https://www.planoly.com/blog/how-to-post-a-trial-reel-on-instagram.md, MEDIUM; Help Center page https://help.instagram.com/1013292530224018/ unreadable). Social Media Today (July 2025) and Publer say 1,000 followers (MEDIUM/LOW). At 448 followers the account passes only the first reading. The cheapest test is one API call with `trial_params` and reading the error.

### 3.2 Publishing through the API (Meta developer docs)

- **[HIGH] Changelog, Dec 3, 2025, "Supporting Trial Reels in the Content Publishing API":** "`trial_params` is optional and part of the create media endpoint." Applies to Instagram API with Facebook Login and with Instagram Login, "All versions." https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/changelog.md
- **[HIGH] Exact parameters.** Endpoint `POST /<IG_ID>/media` with `media_type=REELS` and `video_url`. `trial_params` is an object with one required field, `graduation_strategy`. Allowed values:
  - `MANUAL`: "The trial reel can be manually graduated in the native app."
  - `SS_PERFORMANCE`: "The trial reel will be automatically graduated if the trial reel performs well."
  Reference text: "The `media_type` must be `REELS` if this parameter is included in the request." Values are case-sensitive in at least one wrapper (Ayrshare: https://www.ayrshare.com/apis/post/social-networks/instagram , MEDIUM). Sources: https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media.md and https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/content-publishing.md
- **[HIGH] Docs example (uses v27.0, `graph.instagram.com`; the sample JSON in the docs omits commas, so it is not valid as printed):**
  `curl -X POST "https://graph.instagram.com/v27.0/<IG_ID>/media" -H "Content-Type: application/json" -d '{"media_type":"REELS","video_url":"https://.../x.mp4","trial_params":{"graduation_strategy":"MANUAL"}}'`
  For the Facebook Login flow the host is `graph.facebook.com` with an access token.
- **[HIGH] API version.** No minimum version is stated for `trial_params`; the changelog says "All versions" and dates it Dec 3, 2025, when Graph API v24.0 (released Oct 8, 2025) was current. Meta's versions page (fetched today) lists v22.0 (Jan 21, 2025), v23.0 (May 29, 2025), v24.0 (Oct 8, 2025), v25.0 (Feb 18, 2026) and v26.0 (Jul 29, 2026); the publishing docs' examples already use v27.0, which that page did not list. https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/versions
- **[HIGH] What the docs do NOT give:** no field on IG Media to tell whether a published reel is a trial, and no endpoint to graduate one (https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media.md). Ayrshare says graduation "isn't exposed by Meta's API" and must be done in the app (MEDIUM). So the pipeline must record locally which media IDs were published as trials.
- **[HIGH] Related publishing facts:** `share_to_feed`, `cover_url`, `thumb_offset`, `audio_name`, `collaborators` (up to 3), `user_tags`, `location_id` exist for REELS; "Instagram accounts are limited to 100 API-published posts within a 24-hour moving period" (the same page elsewhere says 50 for carousels). A new `is_ai_generated=true` parameter (Jun 22, 2026) self-discloses AI use. Apps on Facebook Login can attach trending or licensed audio at publish time with `audio_configuration` (section 7).

### 3.3 Evidence that trials are useful
- **[LOW-MEDIUM]** Independent evidence is thin and vendor-run: Buffer's one-video test (three audio versions, a few weeks, no sample size) found trending audio got 24% more views than voice-over and 33% more than ASMR with tied average watch time; the author's trials peaked near 180 views against 1,500-5,000 for regular reels (https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-trial-reels/). Kapwing's two-reel test is summarised at https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-trial-reels-work-research . Planoly's own conclusion: the evidence does not show trials beat regular reels.
- **Design implication (inference, labelled as such):** trials give you a no-follower, same-conditions sample, which is exactly what is needed to calibrate a "fake audience" (section 11).

## 4. Graph API permissions and the current Reels metric names

### 4.1 Permissions for "Instagram API with Facebook Login"
- **[HIGH] Reels insights** (`GET /<IG_MEDIA_ID>/insights`): `instagram_basic`, `instagram_manage_insights`, `pages_read_engagement`; plus `ads_management` and `ads_read` if the app user's Page role comes through Business Manager. Meta lists permissions by login type, not per metric. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media/insights.md
- **[HIGH] Business Discovery** (`business_discovery.username(<name>){media{view_count,like_count,comments_count}}`): the edge reference lists the same set: `instagram_basic`, `instagram_manage_insights`, `pages_read_engagement` (+ `ads_management` or `ads_read` for the Business Manager case). https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/business_discovery.md . Note the IG User node page lists only `instagram_basic` and `pages_read_engagement`, so request all three. Meta's permission glossary describes `instagram_manage_insights` as: "Your app can also discover and read the profile info and media of other business profiles." https://developers.facebook.com/docs/permissions/#instagram_manage_insights
- **[HIGH] Access level:** Advanced Access (App Review, Business Verification) if you serve accounts you don't own or manage; Standard Access if you own or manage them and added them in the App Dashboard. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/insights.md
- **[HIGH] Business Discovery limits:** other accounts' media returns `like_count`, `comments_count`, `view_count`; "`view_count` includes both paid and organic metrics"; age-gated accounts return nothing; `media_url` is omitted for reels whose owner disabled downloads or that use copyrighted or licensed audio. Business Discovery `view_count` (Reels) arrived Jun 16, 2025; since Apr 22, 2026 it returns combined Instagram + Facebook views for crossposted video. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/instagram-api-with-facebook-login/business-discovery.md and the changelog below.
- **[LOW] Terms check:** Meta's Platform Terms contain no express rule on training ML models but restrict processing Platform Data to "permitted purposes set forth in Meta's Developer Docs" (3.a.viii) and treat aggregated or derived data as Platform Data (12.l). Check before building a training set from other accounts' Business Discovery data. https://developers.facebook.com/terms/

### 4.2 Valid REELS insight metrics (reference page says latest version v27.0)

| Metric | Meta's description / status |
|---|---|
| `views` | Total number of times the media has been played on Instagram; "in development" |
| `reach` | Unique accounts that saw the reel at least once; estimated |
| `ig_reels_avg_watch_time` | "The average amount of time spent playing the reel." (unit not stated on the page) |
| `ig_reels_video_view_total_time` | Total play time including replays; "in development" |
| `reels_skip_rate` | Percentage of views skipped in the first 3 seconds; estimated, in development |
| `likes`, `comments`, `shares`, `saved`, `reposts` | counts (`saved`: "Number of time your app user's Instagram media was saved") |
| `total_interactions` | likes + saves + comments + shares minus un-actions; "in development" |
| `crossposted_views`, `facebook_views` | cross-app plays; `crossposted_views` errors if the reel is not shared to Facebook |
| `total_views`, `total_likes`, `total_comments` | Facebook Login only; include promoted/boosted and Facebook |

- **[HIGH] Deprecated:** `plays`, `clips_replays_count`, `ig_reels_aggregated_all_plays_count` and `impressions` (Jan 21, 2025 changelog; v22.0+ immediately, all versions from Apr 21, 2025; `views` was added the same day). `reels_skip_rate` and `reposts` were added Dec 3, 2025. No `follows` or `profile_visits` metric exists for REELS (only FEED and STORY), so follower gain cannot be attributed per reel through the API. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/changelog.md
- **[HIGH] Other 2026 changes that matter:** `media_audio_type` field (`MUSIC` or `ORIGINAL_SOUND`, Jun 1, 2026); `is_ai_generated` readable on IG Media (Jun 22, 2026); `shares_count`, `saved_count`, `reposts_count` on IG Media for Facebook Login apps (Apr 22, 2026). (changelog link above)

## 5. Hooks and the first 1-3 seconds

- **[HIGH] Instagram's own advice.** "Start with a compelling hook in the first three seconds that compels people to stick around" (Feb 2025) and "Pull the viewer in within the first 3 seconds, and keep them interested the whole time" (2022/2023 page). Sources in section 1. The skip metric, the Reels card's "watch less than 3 seconds" prediction and its "skipped within 2 seconds" input all sit on the same window (sections 1.1 and 2).
- **[HIGH, vendor data] How many plays fail the first 3 seconds.** About 60.5% to 65.5% of plays on brand-account Reels are skipped in the first 3 seconds (Socialinsider, Jan-Jun 2026, 9.77M Reels; 65.5% for 1-5K-follower accounts). https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/
- **[MEDIUM] Speech, faces, text, pacing (Emplifi, published Mar 19, 2026).** Facebook Reels from brand pages, not Instagram. The page does not state a sample (a search excerpt said 10,110 Reels from 704 pages; unverified), and the figures are raw differences, not controlled effects. https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/
  - Speech beginning in the first 3 seconds vs music-only: 3-second retention +11.7%, 10-second +24.7%, 30-second +6.6%; sound-on 3-second view ratio +8.8%; engagement rate +5.6%; reach -3.3%; replays -10.6%. 61.8% of brand Reels had no speech.
  - A person visible in the first 3 seconds: 3-second retention +3.6%, 10-second +10.1%, 30-second -2.4%, reach -7.4%.
  - Text overlays: 3-second retention +5.1%, 10-second +7.6%, 30-second about unchanged overall; on speech-led Reels 10-second -6% and 30-second -4.5% (use text lightly when someone is talking).
  - Pacing: little effect on retention overall; slower pace/slower start gave +7.7-7.8% 30-second retention. Vertical vs square/horizontal: reach +20.9%, 30-second retention +38.5%.
- **[LOW-MEDIUM] Early value.** The oft-quoted "47% of a video campaign's value is delivered in the first three seconds, 74% in the first ten" comes from 2016 Facebook-commissioned Nielsen work on feed video ads; I could only see secondary citations (search result, not opened): https://www.smartinsights.com/social-media-marketing/facebook-marketing/facebook-video-advertising-creative-best-practices . A vendor claim that 50-60% of Shorts drop-off happens in the first 3 seconds (OpusClip, YouTube Shorts, internal data) is LOW (search result, not opened): https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention
- **[LOW] Text overlay on Instagram (Fanpage Karma, 1,000 posts, undated, no controls).** Posts with text: +38% reach per follower and +40% engagement, but nearly 60% fewer interactions per view. https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/instagram-text-overlays/
- **[LOW, no data] Motion and the first frame:** I found no quantitative study. Instagram's only related line is about rewatching: "Even something as small as an interesting transition may get people watching more than once." https://creators.instagram.com/reels
- **No independent, peer-reviewed number for first-3-second drop-off on Instagram exists in what I could find.** The Socialinsider skip rate is the best measured one.

## 6. Length: most views vs most watch time

- **[HIGH] Instagram's stated cap:** "In order to be recommended, your content has to be 3 minutes or less" (eligibility page, section 1.3). History: in June 2024 Instagram said it "won't recommend Reels that are over 90 seconds long" (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-on-single-word-ctas-and-longer-reels/718151/); the 3-minute upload limit was announced in January 2025 (Social Media Today gives Jan 18, other outlets Jan 20) and that article noted Instagram's advice was still to stay under 90 seconds (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-3-minutes/737766/, MEDIUM). Treat 90 seconds as the cautious ceiling. Mosseri, as quoted by Buffer: "no one 'perfect' length - the goal is to keep people watching." https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/ (MEDIUM). Watch time counts as both percentage and seconds (section 1.1, MEDIUM).
- **[HIGH, vendor data] Socialinsider (Jan-Jun 2026; 9,771,822 organic Reels; brands with 1K-1M followers; length results are NOT split by account size; per-bracket sample sizes not given):** https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/ and https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/

  | Length | Engagement rate | Median views | Reach rate |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0-30 s | 0.28% | 4,700 | 5.20% (under 30 s) |
  | 30-45 s | 0.30% | 8,564 | 5.60% (30-60 s) |
  | 45-60 s | 0.35% | 10,374 | 5.60% (30-60 s) |
  | 60-90 s | 0.30% | 9,790 | 5.30% |
  | 90-120 s | 0.30% | 8,000 | 4.30% |
  | 120-180 s | 0.33% | 9,000 | 3.50% (over 120 s) |
  | 180 s+ | 0.15% | 4,428 | 3.50% (over 120 s) |

  Engagement = likes + comments / followers. The page calls the views figure both "average" and "median", and bigger accounts tend to post longer, so length is confounded with account size.
- **[HIGH, vendor data] Small-account baseline (Socialinsider):** 975,409 Reels from 58,192 accounts under 5K followers, Jan-Jun 2026: average engagement 0.80%, average views 658 (https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics). 2025 views per Reel for 1-5K accounts: 580 (https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram). Reels reach rate by brand size: 9.78% for 1-5K, 7.55% for 5-10K, 7.10% for 10-50K (the Reels page above).
- **[HIGH, vendor data] Metricool Instagram Study 2026 (24,364,803 posts, 375,118 accounts, Jan-Feb 2025 vs Jan-Feb 2026):** average Reel watch time 8.5 seconds, "more than double" the prior year; the press release does not define it. https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/ . A secondary reading says it is total watch time divided by initial views, so loops can exceed the video's length (LOW). Metricool's 2026 Social Media Study (39,762,999 posts, 1,059,949 accounts, 2025): Reels reach -35% year on year, posts -31%. https://metricool.com/press-release-2026-social-media-study/
- **[LOW] Completion falls with length (Highviz, 346 reels from 108 agency-connected accounts, snapshot 2026-08-20; bracket counts sum to 339):** average watch ratio 66.0% (0-15 s, 121 reels), 31.5% (15-30 s, 90), 22.1% (30-60 s, 67), 16.4% (60 s+, 61); engagement per view 7.3%, 9.1%, 5.3%, 8.1%. https://www.highviz.io/blog/how-long-should-instagram-reels-be/
- **[MEDIUM] Emplifi 2023 (thousands of brand accounts; Instagram Reels):** median views under 30 s 6,145; 30-90 s 7,830; over 90 s 8,372. https://emplifi.io/press/emplifi-reports-instagram-reels-outperformed-other-video-content-instagram-facebook-tiktok
- **Not found:** an Instagram Reels length dataset from Later, Hootsuite, Dash Hudson or Buffer (Buffer's length guide has no data). OpusClip's length data is for YouTube Shorts.
- **Reading across the sources:** views, reach and engagement peak at about 30-60 seconds in the large dataset; the share of the video watched falls steadily as length grows; absolute watch seconds rise with length until retention collapses; Instagram weighs both. No source isolates small accounts, so the 30-45 s the account uses sits inside the best-performing band and a split test is the only way to find its own optimum.

## 7. Captions, on-screen text, music, trending audio, AI voices, AI labels, "looks like an ad"

### 7.1 Captions and on-screen text
- **[HIGH] Instagram:** text should not "cover the majority of the screen"; "reels that are majority text" and "reels that are muted or contain borders" are less likely to be recommended (section 1.3). Put keywords in the caption (not comments); "Search results are matched by text."
- **[HIGH] Hashtags:** posts and Reels are being capped at five hashtags (reported Dec 18, 2025), with Instagram saying "using fewer (up to 5) more targeted hashtags" can improve performance; Mosseri has said hashtags "don't work" to increase reach but help describe a post. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/ (MEDIUM, trade press quoting Instagram)
- **[MEDIUM] Text vs speech:** see section 5 (Emplifi): text helps music-led Reels and slightly hurts speech-led ones at 10 and 30 seconds.
- **[LOW, no controlled data] Caption style and size:** I found no controlled study of animated word-by-word captions vs static subtitles or of font size. Vendor blogs claim 10-28% retention gains for animated captions without methods (e.g. https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/ai-captions-increase-views.html), and a widely repeated "13.5 million clips" prevalence statistic could not be traced to its source. Instagram's only rule is the one above: text must not cover most of the screen. Test one variable at a time.
- **[LOW] Safe zones:** Metricool's guide says keep the top 250 px and bottom 250 px clear (unattributed): https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/ . Other sites quote Meta ad-specs of 14% top / 35% bottom / 6% sides; I could not confirm them against a Meta page. Use the Ads Manager "safe zone" overlay to check.
- **[LOW] Sound on or off:** Mosseri has reportedly said about half of Instagram video impressions are watched with sound off (June 2024, secondhand); other sites cite 20-40% silent with no source, and a 2019 Verizon/Publicis survey (not opened) put muted viewing far higher. I found no reliable Reels-specific number.

### 7.2 Music under voice-over and trending audio
- **[HIGH] Instagram:** "Incorporating audio to reels can bring them to life and grab attention with new audiences" (Feb 2025); visiting a reel's audio page and reusing its audio are modelled actions (2023 post; Reels card).
- **[HIGH] Audio API (changelog Jun 1, 2026; Facebook Login and Unified Login only, not Instagram Login):** `GET /ig_audio` with `audio_type=music` or `original_sound`, plus `user_id` (a call without `search_query` returns trending audio); publish with `audio_configuration` = {`audio_id` (required), `audio_volume` 1-100 (default 100), `video_volume` 1-100 (default 100)}. Music is limited to audio "authorized for third party use", there is no preview, and the app needs `instagram_basic` and `instagram_content_publish`. The doc's examples use v22.0. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/content-publishing/audio-api.md . The `media_audio_type` field (`MUSIC` or `ORIGINAL_SOUND`) can be read back.
- **[LOW-MEDIUM] Does trending audio help?** One Buffer test: trending audio got 24% more views than a voice-over version with tied average watch time (one video, three versions, no sample size; https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-trial-reels/). An agency's comparison of original audio vs named music found no significant difference (engagement 8.00 vs 10.08, p=0.1449; reach 142.87 vs 149.67, p=0.8681; sample not disclosed): https://www.thatrandomagency.com/blog/do-trending-sounds-impact-instagram-reel-performance . The popular "2.5x views" claims could not be traced to a primary study.
- **Music under a voice-over:** I found no controlled study. Treat the `audio_volume` setting as a variable to test.

### 7.3 AI voices and AI labels
- **[HIGH] Meta policy (Feb 6, 2024):** "We'll require people to use this disclosure and label tool when they post organic content with a photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, and we may apply penalties if they fail to do so." https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/ . A realistic synthetic voice-over fits "realistic-sounding audio" on a plain reading; how strictly this is enforced is not published.
- **[HIGH] Label mechanics:** the label is "AI info" (renamed from "Made with AI" on Jul 1, 2024); it is applied when Meta detects industry-standard AI indicators or when people self-disclose; Meta's post does not say labelled content is demoted. https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/
- **[HIGH] API self-disclosure:** `is_ai_generated=true` on the media container (changelog Jun 22, 2026; both login types; "Not available for carousel children"); readable back on IG Media. https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/changelog.md
- **[HIGH] Profile label (Aug 31, 2026):** applies to profiles that "feature an AI-generated person"; "We will also limit the reach of profiles that feature an AI-generated person and do not add this label"; "Creators who simply use AI tools as part of their creative process don't need to add the label"; creators who add it proactively "will not see a change to their profile's reach." The post does not address AI voices, AI video or faceless accounts. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/ai-generated-profile-label . Charts and stick figures are not an "AI-generated person" on a plain reading (my inference; Instagram does not define it).
- **[LOW] Reach effect of the post-level label:** no controlled study exists that I could find; blogs say no penalty is published; anecdotal reports only (search results, e.g. https://www.michydev.com/instagram-ai-content-label-reach/).
- **[LOW] ElevenLabs watermark:** ElevenLabs says its audio carries imperceptible watermarks that its own detector reads (https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/audio-tools/audio-detector); I found no statement that Instagram reads them.
- **Audience research on AI voice (all short-video platforms other than Instagram):**
  - **[MEDIUM]** ICIS 2023 (Luo, Zheng, Yin, Teo; preliminary results): 21,541 TikTok videos from 3,647 creators; AI-generated voice cut likes by 5.4%, comments by 5.2% and shares by 7.4%, most at the "rising action" stage. https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2023/user_behav/user_behav/24
  - **[MEDIUM]** UBC (Zhang, Zhou, Lee): 554,252 TikTok videos; adopting TikTok's AI voice raised output 21.8% (news release: 24% more videos a week); engagement rose for less-experienced creators and fell for experienced ones and those with established identities. https://blogs.ubc.ca/genemoolee/2022/07/06/how-does-ai-generated-voice-affect-online-content-creation-evidence-from-text-to-speech-adoption-in-tiktok/ and https://news.ubc.ca/2024/05/tiktok-users-more-productive-and-negative-when-they-use-ai-voice/
  - **[LOW-MEDIUM]** HICSS 2025: 129 Bilibili videos; AI voice promoted videos less well than human voice. https://citation.thinkst.com/talk/95570
  - **[LOW]** An International Journal of Information Management 2025 paper ("Voices that captivate") reportedly found AI voice-overs drew less engagement on TikTok, with a celebrity AI voice matching human voices and lower pitch narrowing the gap; I could not open it (HTTP 403) and know it only from a search summary: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0268401225000945
- **Net:** Instagram says nothing about AI voices in ranking; independent TikTok data leans negative for established creators, neutral-to-positive for newcomers; nothing is measured on ElevenLabs-quality voices on Instagram.

### 7.4 "Looks like an ad"
- **[LOW, no official statement]** No official Instagram statement says organic content that looks like an ad is down-ranked. The nearest official signals are the survey ("worth their time or fun"), the Recommendation Guidelines (clickbait, engagement bait, contests and giveaways) and the border/muted/majority-text rules. ## 8. Endings: loops, CTAs, end cards

- **[HIGH] Instagram:** "Plan a big reveal, add a punchline or end with a plot twist." "Even something as small as an interesting transition may get people watching more than once." https://creators.instagram.com/reels
- **[MEDIUM] Loops (Emplifi, Facebook, videos up to 7 seconds only):** seamless loops raised replay rate +18.7%, reach +23.6% and engagement rate +16.1%; music-only videos had 11.9% higher replay rates than speech videos. https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/ . Not tested on 30-45 s Reels.
- **[HIGH] Replays count in watch-time metrics:** `ig_reels_video_view_total_time` is "The total amount of time the reel was played, including any time spent replaying the reel", and `total_views_count` includes replays (section 4 links). Highviz notes watch ratios above 100% occur on rewatched reels (LOW). So a reveal that invites a second watch can lift average watch time.
- **[HIGH] CTAs (Instagram, June 5, 2024, via Social Media Today):** "There haven't been changes to our recommendation guidelines"; creators can still use tools such as ManyChat; "earnestly engaging" in comments and DMs is not engagement bait; the target is content that tries to "game the system" by eliciting specific interactions. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-on-single-word-ctas-and-longer-reels/718151/ . Engagement bait and "contests and giveaways" are on the not-recommended list (section 1.3, MEDIUM).
- **Link in bio and native links [HIGH that the product exists; region and rollout may vary]:** Meta One plans (Sept 15, 2026): Essential ($14.99/month) includes a link page of up to 20 links; Advanced ($49.99/month) adds native links on four posts and four reels per month. The page says nothing about reach effects. https://creators.instagram.com/blog/meta-one-creator-plans
- **Do end cards or sign-offs hurt average watch time?** No study found. The only claims are vendor opinions that conflict (CTA near 85% of runtime vs looping): https://www.terramarketgroup.com/blog/short-form-video-retention-hooks-2026-a-data-informed-framework-for-higher-completion-rates and https://www.trymypost.com/blog/youtube-shorts-end-screen-strategy-2026 (LOW; seen in search results, not opened; the second is about YouTube Shorts).
- **Mechanism worth knowing (inference):** skip rate covers only seconds 0-3, so an ending cannot raise or lower it; an ending can only affect average watch time, completion and replays. Test an ending by comparing `ig_reels_avg_watch_time` divided by duration between two versions of the same reel.

## 9. Posting frequency and timing for small accounts

- **[HIGH] Instagram:** "One of the most effective ways to grow is to post consistently" (Feb 2025 post).
- **[MEDIUM] Mosseri, Reel of early Dec 2024 (RouteNote summary):** no single right frequency; examples range from one post a month to five Reels a day; more posting is linked to more reach, with a burnout warning. https://routenote.com/blog/how-often-you-should-post-on-instagram/
- **[MEDIUM] Sept 2026 series:** Social Media Today says creators "look to post as much as they can to increase their chances of having a hit, while also managing the time they have to avoid burnout" (the article's wording, not a Mosseri quote; no cadence given): https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-shares-algorithm-insights-and-posting-tips/831326/ . Parasocial Magazine says Mosseri called the fear that frequent posting is penalised misplaced (I could not find that line in Social Media Today's text; LOW-MEDIUM): https://www.parasocialmagazine.com/news/mosseri-names-four-instagram-ranking-principles-for-creators/
- **[LOW-MEDIUM] July 2026 AMA:** posting a carousel and a Reel on the same day "won't impact reach" (newsletter paraphrase): https://thesocialtea.beehiiv.com/p/does-posting-twice-on-instagram-in-a-day-reduce-reach
- **[HIGH, vendor data] Buffer (published Aug 13, 2025; 2.1M posts from 102K accounts; formats mixed, Stories excluded; reach-per-post results not size-adjusted):** compared with 1-2 posts a week, 3-5 gave about 12% more reach per post, 6-9 about 18% and 10+ about 24%, "the returns are diminishing"; Buffer recommends 3-5 a week. The study does not cover more than 10 a week or posts per day. https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/
- **[HIGH, vendor data] Socialinsider (H1 2026):** average Reels per month: 8 (1-5K followers), 10 (5-10K), 13 (10-50K), 17 (50-100K), 20 (100K-1M). Seven a day is about 210 a month, roughly 25 times the small-account average. https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/
- **[MEDIUM] Saturation:** Reels reach fell 35% in 2025 while publishing grew (Metricool, section 6).
- **[HIGH/MEDIUM] Recency matters:** nearly 45% of recommended Reels were under a day old in Q3 2026 (Meta call); same-day posts were more than 30% of recommended Reels in Q1 2026 (MediaNama); recency is one of Mosseri's four named principles (Sept 2026).
- **Limits you will hit:** API publishing cap 100 posts per 24 hours (HIGH); Trial Reels cap 20 per day (MEDIUM, section 3).
- **Best-time studies conflict and are small effects:** Buffer 2025 (2M+ posts, reach, weekday afternoons) vs Buffer 2026 (9.6M posts, engagement, Thursday 9 a.m., Wednesday 12 p.m. and 6 p.m.) vs Later (6M+ posts Jan-Oct 2024, Reels sample ~975K, best 12 AM Monday; for 1-5K accounts, weekdays 5 p.m.). LOW-MEDIUM. https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram and https://later.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram/
- **Does 7 a day help or hurt?** No source tests it. What is established: Instagram does not say it is penalised; per-post reach in Buffer's data rose with weekly volume up to 10+ (not Reels-specific); each Reel gets its own small non-follower test, so more distinct tries means more chances of a breakout if quality holds. What is not established: whether near-identical templated posts split or dilute the same test audience, whether Meta's new "ranks sequences of Reels at a time" model limits repeated creators, and whether an account's recent hit rate affects the next reel's test audience.

## 11. Predicting short-video engagement before posting, and LLM "fake audiences"

### 11.1 What content alone can predict (supervised, real outcomes)
- **[MEDIUM] Early retention (watching past 5 seconds) can be predicted from content to about 0.7 rank correlation when trained at scale.** The ICCV 2025 VQualA / SnapUGC challenge used 120,651 short videos (106,192 train, 6,000 validation, 8,459 test) with the label Engagement Continuation Rate = "the proportion of viewers who watched the video for at least 5 seconds." Baseline SROCC about 0.66 (PLCC 0.657); VideoLLaMA2 (frames, title, description and audio) 0.691 SROCC; Qwen2.5-VL (no audio) 0.665-0.674; the winning ensemble 0.707 SROCC / 0.714 PLCC. Audio features were the stated reason the audio-aware model won. These models were trained on 106K videos, so this is a ceiling for a mature supervised system, not a zero-shot result. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02969 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02516 (and the 0.707 row at https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02516v2)
- **[MEDIUM] Video-to-text plus an LLM works only with help (Kayal et al., Findings of ACL 2025).** Task: a roughly balanced two-class split of YouTube trending videos into "global big hit" (top 25% on both view count and number of countries) vs "local hit" (bottom 25% on both), 13,639 videos. A supervised neural net on content embeddings: 80% accuracy; an LLM without fine-tuning: 82%; the two combined: 85.5%. The prompt ladder matters: a vanilla prompt scored 59.0% (title only) to 62.5% (video-to-text description); adding retrieved nearest labelled examples lifted it to 68.5-72.0%; hypothesis generation plus a supervised signal reached about 79-82%. Caveat: it separates extremes among already-popular videos, which is far easier than ranking similar reels. https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.597/
- **[MEDIUM] Training on behaviour:** Large Content and Behavior Models (Adobe et al., ICLR 2024) fine-tune an LLM with receiver "behavior tokens" (likes, shares, views) and report beating 3-shot GPT-3.5 and 10-shot GPT-4 on behaviour-simulation tasks such as predicting YouTube replay values and likes-to-views ratio (details from the abstract and a search summary; I did not read the tables). https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.00359
- **[HIGH, classic] Hard limits on predicting hits:** in a 14,341-participant music market the best songs rarely did poorly and the worst rarely did well, but anything in between was possible (Salganik, Dodds, Watts, Science 2006). https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/303s19/docs/salganik_dodds_watts.pdf . Instagram says the same of its own system: "Recommendations make it more difficult to predict the reach of every post" (section 1.1).

### 11.3 Design questions

- **Pairwise vs absolute scores.** For LLM judges, pairwise preferences align better with humans than direct scores and calibration alone does not repair direct scoring (Liu et al., COLM 2024: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16950 , MEDIUM; cost grows quadratically with candidates). A multimodal-judge benchmark found pair comparison close to human judgement but scoring and batch ranking diverged (MLLM-as-a-Judge, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04788 , search summary, MEDIUM). Asking for a numeric rating gives unrealistic distributions (SSR above). But the engagement-specific evidence shows pairwise form alone is not enough: zero-shot LLMs are near chance on which headline wins (LOLA, Clark, Maiorano). I found no paper that tests pairwise multimodal-LLM judging of which of two short videos gets more views.
- **Position bias.** GPT-4-class judges flip with answer order; the original MT-Bench paper reported 65.0% consistency when order was swapped, raised to 77.5% with few-shot examples (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05685 , numbers from a search summary; overall >80% agreement with human preferences on chat tasks, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685 , MEDIUM). Standard fix: run both orders and count only agreement.
- **Persona panels.** Mixed. They hurt against a generic reader on headline ranking (Maiorano), explain little variance (Hu and Collier), flatten groups (Wang et al.), yet demographic conditioning mattered for purchase-intent ranking (SSR) and personas from real behaviour data reached 0.75-0.80 directional accuracy (Benomar). Reading: personas help only when grounded in real audience data and when checked against a no-persona baseline on your own outcomes.
- **Calibration.** The one feedback-loop study (Clark) gained most from the first round of real outcomes; SSR calibrates the response scale; Bojic et al. show model choice swings accuracy by 13 points and Bisbee et al. show drift across months. Pin model versions and re-score a held-out set regularly.
- **Inputs.** Frame descriptions plus transcript plus audio features worked (ACL 2025; VQualA winners used audio), and retrieving similar past items with known outcomes gave the largest prompt-only gain (+6 to +10 points in the ACL paper).
- **What to predict.** Early retention (watch past 5 s, here a proxy for skip rate) is learnable from content (SROCC about 0.7 after training on 106K videos); likes and total views are partly luck, on the evidence of Salganik et al. and Instagram's own caveat (my reading).
- **Using simulated predictions in tests.** A 2026 workshop paper shows persona predictions can be combined with real data to cut A/B-test sample sizes while staying valid even if the predictions are poor (prediction-powered inference): https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24629 (MEDIUM-LOW, abstract only).
- **Instagram's own use of LLMs:** Meta says it processes public Instagram posts "each day through LLM-based content-understanding systems" (Q3 2026 call, HIGH), so a clear, topic-legible reel is also legible to the ranker.

### 11.4 Known to work / known not to work (from the evidence above)

Works (with the size of the gain):
- Training or calibrating on real outcomes; about 2x chance on decisive headline pairs after retraining on revealed results (Clark); about 0.7 SROCC for early retention with 100K+ training videos (VQualA).
- Free-text responses mapped by embeddings instead of asking for numbers (SSR).
- Content as text (frames + transcript) plus audio, with retrieval of similar labelled examples (ACL 2025).
- Personas built from real behavioural data (Benomar), judged against a generic baseline.
- Pairwise with both orderings; ensembles across models (my inference from the 13-point model spread).

Does not work or is unproven:
- Zero-shot prompting to pick winners (LOLA, Clark, Qiu).
- Demographic persona panels without a baseline check (Maiorano; Hu and Collier).
- Treating LLM numeric scores as calibrated (SSR; MT-Bench position bias).
- Expecting to rank near-identical candidates: even the best calibrated model was right about half the time on decisive pairs (Clark 52.8%).
- Using simulated audiences as a replacement for testing (Clark's own conclusion; Instagram's reach is "difficult to predict").
- Unknown: any validated simulator for Instagram Reels retention curves; I found none.

### 11.5 What this means for a fake audience at @memestreetgame (inference, not a finding)
- Use it as a reject-the-worst filter on hooks and scripts, not as a winner picker.
- Predict `reels_skip_rate`, average watch time divided by duration, and shares per reach, not likes.
- Ground-truth supply: about 200 posts a month, published as Trial Reels, gives labelled outcomes at 24-72 hours, enough for light calibration (rank correlation, top-third precision) but not for training a model of the VQualA kind.
- First experiment: score each batch with (a) a generic-reader prompt, (b) persona prompts built from your own Insights audience data and real comments, in both pairwise orders; keep personas only if they beat (a) on your own Spearman against real skip rate.

## 12. Top 15 actionable changes for @memestreetgame, ranked by expected impact

Items 3 and 13 are prerequisites for measuring the rest. "Evidence" says how strong the support is; items marked hypothesis rest on inference.

1. **Rebuild seconds 0-3 of every format.** Put the stake on screen and in the voice at t=0: chart plus question visible on frame 1, narration starting on the first frame, no brand header or logo bar, intro, greeting or silence, first-frame text of a few large words, readable with the sound off. *Why:* Instagram's hook advice (sections 1.1, 5), skip rate is defined on seconds 0-3 and Meta's card uses a 2-second skip and "watch less than 3 seconds" (sections 1.1, 2), about 60-65% of plays on brand accounts are skipped there (section 2), speech in the first 3 seconds lifted 3-second and 10-second retention in Emplifi's Facebook data (section 5). *Test:* `reels_skip_rate` against your own trailing median; beat the 65.5% small-brand average first. *Evidence:* strong that early seconds decide distribution; moderate on specific tactics.
2. **Design for sends per reach, not likes.** Build in a reason to forward the reel to a friend (a surprising answer, a "most people get this wrong" chart, a relatable skit line) instead of a begging CTA. *Why:* with 448 followers the audience is unconnected, where Mosseri says sends are slightly more important than likes (1.1-1.2); Reels are the share-and-comment format (10). *Test:* `shares` divided by `reach` per format. *Evidence:* moderate; no finance-quiz data.
3. **Publish new formats and hook variants as Trial Reels through the API and compare trial with trial.** Use `trial_params.graduation_strategy` = `SS_PERFORMANCE` (auto-graduate) or `MANUAL`; store trial media IDs yourself because the API has no trial flag or graduate call. *Why:* section 3 (non-follower-only audience, Mosseri: compare trials with trials). *Check first:* eligibility at 448 followers (200 vs 1,000 conflict) by sending one request. *Evidence:* strong on mechanics, weak on proof that trials beat normal posts.
4. **Flatten the retention curve.** One question, one payoff; tease early, reveal in the last third; delete dead air; use the in-app Retention chart to find the cliff after each batch; judge by average watch time divided by duration. *Why:* watch time counts as percentage and seconds (1.1); typical watch ratio falls with length (6); average Reel watch time is only about 8.5 seconds across brand accounts (6). *Evidence:* moderate.
5. **Make every reel legible to a cold audience.** Open with the real-world concept in plain words ("stock chart", "support level", "short squeeze") in first-frame text, the spoken line and the caption; add at most five targeted hashtags; do not let a fictional company name be the only hook. *Why:* the first audience is chosen as "people that we think will enjoy it"; reels "should make sense to those who may not know you"; keywords in captions are matched as text; Meta runs LLM content understanding on public posts daily (1.1, 1.3, 7.1, 10). *Evidence:* moderate (official guidance, no measured effect size).
6. **Turn volume into controlled experiments and space the posts.** Keep high output only if each post is a logged variant (same script, one changed variable), and spread posts so each gets its own test window. *Why:* no source tests 7 posts a day; Buffer shows rising reach per post up to 10+ posts a week with diminishing returns (not Reels-specific); the account posts about 25 times the small-account average; about 45% of recommended Reels are under a day old (9). *Evidence:* weak on volume itself, so measure it.
7. **Test the voice and decide on AI disclosure.** Run the same script with (a) the current ElevenLabs voice, (b) a more human-paced or lower-pitched voice and (c) a human read, as trial pairs; separately test `is_ai_generated=true` on a subset. *Why:* TikTok data shows AI voice lowering likes and shares by 5-7% on average but helping new creators (7.3); Meta's policy asks for disclosure of realistic synthetic audio and says it may penalise non-disclosure, while no reach penalty for the label is published (7.3); the Aug 2026 profile rule targets AI-generated people, not charts or stick figures (inference). *Evidence:* weak-moderate; nothing measured on Instagram.
8. **Keep 30-45 s as the default but split-test 20-30 s and 45-55 s cuts of the same script.** *Why:* views, reach and engagement peak around 30-60 s in 9.77M Reels, completion ratio falls with length, Instagram counts both (6). Go no longer than 60 s and treat 90 s as the cautious ceiling. *Evidence:* moderate; no small-account split.
9. **Enforce Instagram's quality gates in the template.** 1080x1920, no borders, letterboxing or framed panels around the video, no watermark or logo bug, text never the majority of the screen, captions inside safe zones, audible audio; for finance, no return promises, giveaways or clickbait; check Settings > Account > Account Status monthly. *Why:* sections 1.3, 7.1. If the video frames (not only the cover) use the November template's framed-panel layout, check it against the "no borders" rule. *Evidence:* strong that these are stated criteria; unknown how often they bind.
10. **Add a low music bed through the Audio API and test it.** Facebook-Login apps can `GET /ig_audio` (no query returns trending) and publish with `audio_configuration` {`audio_id`, `audio_volume`, `video_volume`}; keep the voice at 100 and the music low. *Why:* Instagram says audio helps reach new audiences; audio reuse and audio-page visits are modelled actions (7.2). Effect size of trending audio is unproven (one Buffer test +24% views, one agency test not significant). *Evidence:* weak; cheap to test.
11. **Rework the ending.** Drop the sign-off or brand card; end on the reveal or a twist and make the last frame lead back to the first (test a true seamless loop on 15-second variants); say the site name once before the reveal or leave it to the caption and profile; no "comment YES" bait. *Why:* Instagram's "plot twist" and "watching more than once" lines, replays counting in watch time, Emplifi's loop numbers for videos up to 7 s, Instagram's engagement-bait stance (8). No study shows end cards hurt average watch time. *Evidence:* weak for 30-45 s reels.
12. **Build the fake audience as a calibrated reject-the-worst filter.** Video-to-text (frame captions plus transcript plus audio notes), a generic-reader baseline, personas only if they beat it, free-text reactions mapped by embeddings, pairwise comparisons in both orders, retrieval of similar past reels with outcomes, pinned model versions, scored weekly against real skip rate and shares per reach. *Why:* section 11. Expect modest rank correlation (the cited correlations are 0.29-0.37) and use it to discard the bottom third. *Evidence:* moderate on method, weak on achievable accuracy.
13. **Instrument the metrics Instagram optimises.** Pull `reels_skip_rate`, `ig_reels_avg_watch_time`, `views`, `reach`, `shares`, `saved`, `likes`, `comments`, `reposts`, `total_interactions` at 24 h and 72 h; compute skip rate, average watch divided by duration, shares/reach, saves/reach, likes/reach (Mosseri: judge by rates, not totals). Permissions: `instagram_basic`, `instagram_manage_insights`, `pages_read_engagement`. Several metrics are flagged "estimated" or "in development", can lag up to 48 hours and return an empty set instead of 0 when unavailable; there is no per-reel follow count. *Why:* sections 1.2, 2, 4. *Evidence:* strong.
14. **Give the account a recurring identity for return viewers (hypothesis).** One named character or series, a fixed daily slot for the recap, the same look, and a spoken reason to follow ("new chart every day"). *Why:* Instagram says posting consistently is one of the most effective ways to grow and that non-followers who see a reel may become followers (Feb 2025); the only support for recurring characters is a vendor case (10). *Evidence:* weak; measure follows from profile insights in the app.
15. **Use Business Discovery to benchmark niche reels, not to train models.** `business_discovery.username(<name>){media{view_count,like_count,comments_count}}` returns another professional account's per-reel views (paid plus organic) with the same three permissions; use it to set realistic view-to-follower targets and spot format patterns. Check the Platform Terms before using the data to train anything (4.1). *Evidence:* strong on the API, low impact on views.
