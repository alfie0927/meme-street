# The niche, watched: what makes a short money video travel (17 November 2026)

The owner: "spend a couple of hours going through hundreds/thousands of reels and tiktoks in my niche and learn what you
can about what makes success... find the best formula." This is that study. Its rules went into the writer the same night
(`src/make_spec.py`, `src/reel.html`, `src/one_off_ideas.json`, `src/research.py`); the numbers below are the evidence
behind each one, so the next study can check them rather than start again.

## What was done

- **Breadth.** 90 YouTube searches across the whole niche (trading, investing, candlesticks, indicators, scams, crashes,
  company stories, money mistakes, finance comedy, POV skits, game shows, tier lists, economics, crypto-adjacent) gave
  654 Shorts; the 125 channels behind them were listed to their newest 40 Shorts each; every video was ranked against
  **its own channel's median** (the only multiple that says anything copyable). 3,556 Shorts ranked, 92 channels with
  enough history for a median; 161 at 10x or more, 290 at 5x, 510 at 3x, 401 at 0.3x or less. Adverts (banks, mutual
  funds, brokers, a theme park, a toy brand) and off-niche comedy channels pulled in by the "skit" searches were cut
  from every count below.
- **TikTok.** 56 handles tried, 21 accounts listed (983 videos, the newest 60 each, with likes, comments, reposts and
  saves per 1,000 views, which TikTok shows and Instagram hides). Mostly personal-finance creators; the trading-specific
  accounts on TikTok are few and small.
- **Depth.** 111 videos chosen to watch: the 57 YouTube outliers at 4x or more (at most three per channel), 14 flops
  under 0.3x from channels with a real median, 22 TikTok outliers, 6 TikTok flops, the 6 most saved and 6 most reposted
  TikToks. Each was fetched small, cut into one sheet of twelve frames (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 and 12 s, then the
  quarter points and the last frame), measured (hard cuts and visible changes per 10 s, words per second, the second of
  the first word) and transcribed (faster-whisper small.en); the sheets were read one by one. 71 YouTube and 30 TikTok
  files came through (TikTok refused 10). The top comments of 26 YouTube winners were read too.
- **Ours, the same way.** Our 12 best and 12 worst reels by hold were measured with the same script (`ours.json`).
- **The account's own numbers** (the morning's plateau read): reach in the last 29 days 12,214 people, 8 of them
  followers; views stop growing after a reel's first day; the breakouts held 64%+ past 3 seconds with 30%+ watched.

Nothing was kept: the videos were deleted after their frames and words were taken; the scripts live in this session's
scratchpad and `/root/niche-watch` on the server holds only JSON and the frame sheets.

## The numbers that frame everything

| | Winners watched (YouTube, 4x to 6,819x) | Flops watched (0.1x to 0.3x) | Ours (best 12 / worst 12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| First spoken word | 0.0 s in every voiced winner | 0.0 s too | 0.15 s |
| Words per second | 2.8 to 4.2 (Rayner Teo 4.9) | 2.0 to 3.3 | 3.0 both |
| Hard cuts per 10 s | 0 to 1.3 in nearly all; 2 to 6 only in skits and news | 0 to 4.5 | 0 (the two 3D story parts: 5.4) |
| Length | 5 s loops to 118 s; the 33x volume lesson is 106 s | 38 to 102 s | 47 / 57 s |
| Title words | "secret/trick/hack" 29% reach 3x against 13% base; questions and "you/your" in titles BELOW base on both platforms | | |
| TikTok engagement ratios vs multiple | flat: likes, comments, reposts and saves per 1,000 views do not predict the multiple | | |

So: pace, cuts, length and the words of the title do **not** separate winners from flops. What the viewer sees in the
first second, whether the claim stays on screen, and whether something resolves at the end, do.

## What the winners share (the frame sheets)

1. **One subject fills the frame from frame 0, and labels land on it as the words are said.** One giant candle with
   OPEN, CLOSE, HIGH, LOW arriving one by one (43x). One chart with "Volume Indicator" / "Common Misconception" /
   "Volume Decreasing" landing in turn (33x, and it is 106 s long). Two stick figures with one caps word above them per
   beat (100x on a 1,700-view channel). A product held up with three price tags landing over three store logos (28x).
2. **The claim or the subject stays on screen the whole reel.** A fixed title ("Volume POC Flip", "HAMMER CANDLESTICK
   PATTERN", "The Great Refinancing Crisis Is Coming Due", "W & M Pattern: 90% Traders Trapped", "A 90% Win Rate Won't
   Make You a Great Trader") sat at the top of nine of the watched winners for their whole length. Our reels showed the
   hook for three seconds and then only per-scene punches.
3. **Captions are big, central and two to four words at a time**, often one word, often one word coloured. Ours were
   42 px at the bottom, a clause at a time.
4. **The first sentence names one concrete thing** and is a claim, a dare, a command or a naive question: "Don't buy it,
   but put an order in" (47x, 13M), "Stop using RSI on its default settings" (28x), "Wait, isn't charity supposed to help
   people?" (100x), "If I only had 60 seconds to reach every unprofitable trader" (66x), "Would you quit if this happened
   to you?" (7x with 139 comments on 31k views), "This is the exact setup I look for every morning after 15 years" (10x,
   622 comments). A number or a name in the first line in most of them ($700 billion, 5/9/14, Bill Gates, Friday).
5. **Something resolves on screen near the end**: the green/red trade box fills (seven winners), a sum closes ("$50
   million" struck out, "$15 million" in green), a verdict lands, "TARGET HIT".
6. **Exact, testable, arguable content.** Named settings (5, 9, 14), named tools, a precise claim ("Friday's high under
   Thursday's means Monday revisits Friday's low"), a misconception corrected ("the colour of the volume bar doesn't
   matter, the size does"). The comments under these argue, correct and test; that is where the comment counts come
   from. A bare "comment TIME" on a video with nothing to decide was a 0.1x flop; "COMMENT THE WORD VWAP" on a video
   that had just shown an exact setup got 622 comments.
7. **A face reacting** doubles a chart: the young trader celebrating then with his head in his hands over the chart
   that hit the stop by a hair and ran to target (139 comments on 31k views).
8. **Dense reference cards travel without a voice**: a silent 5-second card of nine candle patterns with BUY/SELL
   badges, 200k views on a 17,500 median; a three-candle pattern drawn in three frames, 166k. Made to be paused and
   saved.
9. **The small channels that broke out did it with the same moves** (Sticky Academy 100x on 1,700; Trading Data 57x on
   821; Traders Monopoly 28x on 998; Unger 47x on 422; Day Trading Addict 66x on 4,200; legacyinvestingshow 29x on 123
   on TikTok): one concrete named thing, the claim on screen, the number worked through.

## What the flops share

- A greeting or a mood before the point ("Hey there, my friends, I hope you're having a beautiful, peaceful day": 0.1x
  on a channel whose silent 5-second cards do 10x, in the very same visual style).
- A definition or an abstract topic with no number and no outcome ("Global diversification", "time affluence",
  "the famous Satyam scam explained").
- Cinematic footage of people being wise (two old men in coats, aphorisms, 0.1x on 94,500).
- AI-generated animation ending in an advert (the Pixar-style couple, 0.1x on an indicator channel).
- A format the channel's audience did not come for (a skit on an indicator channel).
- Length without a turn: 73 to 102 s of one voice, no label landing, no box filling.

## TikTok (what is different there)

Thirty TikToks watched (the trading-specific accounts there are few and small; most of the sample is personal finance,
the adjacent niche the ig-viral skill says formats get borrowed from).

- **The same formula, with props.** Mark Tilbury's "Buying GTA 6 can actually save you $5,000" (10.3M, 8.2x, 5,212
  comments): the game box held up on frame 0, the number typed on a calculator and held to camera, the price on a card
  reader, the sum worked across four locations in 29 s and closed on the calculator again. "If you're really a
  millionaire, sell me this pen" (20.3M, 16x): a challenge as the first line, a dialogue, "$5" as the turn.
- **Two columns compared, with tags stacking.** Queenie Tan's mortgage reel (18.9x, 151k on 8,000): a fixed headline
  ("Pay off mortgage fast *without being rich*"), the same person twice under "No offset account / Offset account",
  and a tag landing under each as it is said ($750,000 mortgage, 6% / 6.15%, $50,000 into...), ending "After 30 years /
  After 26 years" with the two totals. The same shape at 20.7x: "What a $280,000 household income buys us", line items
  stacking. This is our Same-money and Would-you-rather idea, drawn as a ledger.
- **Numbers floating above characters that change over time.** Erika Kullberg's loyalty skit (1.9M, 5.7x): "$50k" above
  each head, a "*3 years later*" card, "$53k" against "$90k", then a spreadsheet of both paths year by year.
- **An exact, odd number as the headline, then the sum written by hand.** johnefinance (81x, 104k on a 1,287 median,
  175 comments): "$3,227,500 THE PERFECT RETIREMENT NUMBER" held over the face for 6 s, then $98,900 + $32,200 =
  $131,100, / 4%, circled. A small account, the whole formula in one reel.
- **Dense static cards travel here too.** RiskReward (Norway, 11.5x, 138 comments): a text card of a compounding sum
  ("10,000 a month at 8% reaches 1 million in 6.5 years, 2 million 4 years later, 3 million 3 years after that") held for
  5 s over a jet-ski clip. Nothing moves but the water.
- **Named lists with exact figures.** Humphrey Yang's "the only 4 index funds" (7.4x, 513 comments): "#1: $VTI" tags with
  a screenshot card held up and the return to two decimals; the comments argue the list.
- **What TikTok's extra numbers say.** Likes, comments, reposts and saves per 1,000 views do not predict the multiple
  (flat across quartiles). The most saved per view were travel and freebie lists (72 saves per 1,000), the most reposted
  were "send this to your friends" freebie reels; neither shape is ours. Adverts (#Ad, partner codes) reach 100x+ on
  paid distribution and were cut from every count.
- **Flops there:** news read straight ("Elon Musk is once again making headlines", 0.1x on TradingView's account), a
  freebie list on a finance account (0.1x), a creator's off-topic brag (0.1x).

## What this changed in our reels (the same night)

- **A headline that stays** (`"headline"` in the spec, 3 to 7 words, the claim the reel proves): `reel.html` draws it
  at the top of the title band (y 384-444) from scene 1 to the end of every director reel; the writer's HUD moved under
  it (y 450-520); `check()` refuses a reel without one, or one that just repeats the title or the hook.
- **Bigger captions, fewer words at a time**: caption groups of 22/30 characters (were 30/40) at 50 px (was 42).
- **The writer's rules** (`FIRST3`, the sends-and-comments paragraph): one concrete named thing in the first sentence;
  a claim, dare, command or naive question; the subject fills the frame and its labels land as spoken; the reel ends by
  proving the headline; the kinds of question that drew comments (a decision with no right answer, a disputed number, a
  testable claim, a face reacting to a result) and the kind that flopped (a bare keyword).
- **Evidence the writer reads** (`STUDY_EVIDENCE`, eleven rows with their multiples) for the fun formats, the one-offs
  and the deep formats.
- **Nine new one-off seeds** in `src/one_off_ideas.json` (reaction cam, exact setting, sixty seconds, before/after,
  the exact-setup ritual, entities with eyes, finance-bro parody, prop-and-calculator, numbers over heads) and
  refreshed evidence on five old ones (dense wall, clueless stand-in, fall of, defy the rule, same money).
- **The weekly research list** (`src/research.py`) gained the small channels whose outliers travelled.
- Earlier the same day, from the owner and from our own ledger: nothing overlaps (dry run + critic), a hook formula in
  turn per reel, the learning loop answering "what held them" and "what kept them" separately, the plateau read.

## What to watch next

- The headline and the bigger captions are the two visible changes: read the next ten reels' hold and watched% on the
  scoreboard against the 49.1 median. If hold rises and watched% does not, the body is still the problem.

- A reference-card reel (silent, dense, 5 to 8 s) is the one shape here we have not tried in any form; it is in the
