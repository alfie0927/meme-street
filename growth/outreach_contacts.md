# Who to write to: societies, teachers' gatekeepers and game directories

Researched on 17 November 2026 from public pages, for the sends in `growth/sends.md` and the offer in
`growth/schools.md`. Every contact is one a society or organisation publishes as its own; where a page showed only
a student's personal address, the page is given instead. Check each page on the day: committees change every year.

## Read this first: what the research found about UK schools

**No evidence was found that any UK school or sixth-form college pays for a stock market simulator or competition.**

- The main UK school competition, the Student Investor Challenge (studentinvestor.org), is free to enter, uses live
  London prices and had 9,800 schools and 33,000 pupils in 2020.
- The only UK payments found: one school partnership paid £300 for a Stock Market Challenge licence in 2019, and
  teachers buy a £10 simulator resource on Tes.
- The rest is free or sponsored: HowTheMarketWorks (free with ads), the Wharton competition (free), the FT's free
  "Investment game" lesson, university outreach days.
- The paid examples are American: about US$10 to $15 a student a year (PersonalFinanceLab), US$10 to $30 a team
  (the Stock Market Game).

So the priced offer to UK teachers is a long shot, and the test is still worth its hour a week because it settles
the question. Two adjustments: write to the societies first (they are reachable and several already run trading
games), and if the UK emails bring no question about price by mid-December, send the last 20 to US high-school
personal-finance teachers, where per-student budgets exist.
