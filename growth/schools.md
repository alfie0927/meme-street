# Schools and students: the lesson plan and where to send it

For the roadmap's reach track (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, RC7). You send these from your own accounts. I have not
seen each site's upload form, so check the fields and the rules on the day.

**The file:** `growth/lesson_plan.pdf`, one A4 page, made from `growth/lesson_plan.html` (the command to rebuild
it is in a comment at the top of that file). The lesson is one two-minute game on the projector, voted on by the
class, so it needs no accounts and no student gives a name.

**Why one screen.** Without an account only three visitors a day can play from one internet address
(`daily_visits_per_ip`), and a school usually shares one address. A class on separate devices would hit that
after three students. The plan says so in its "Before you teach it" box. If teachers ask for whole-class play,
that number is the setting to raise.

**One decision for you.** The page's last line says "You may copy and share this page for teaching". OER Commons
asks for a licence on upload; "CC BY" (anyone may reuse it with your name on it) is the usual choice for a free
resource. Pick what you are happy with; change that line in the HTML if you want other words.

## The listing texts

**Title:**
```
Two minutes on a chart: a lesson on risk, reward and why nobody knows the next price
```

**Short description:**
```
A 45-minute lesson for ages 14 to 18. The class plays one two-minute stock chart game together on the projector, voting on each trade, then discusses why prices are hard to predict and how feelings change money decisions. One computer, no accounts, practice money only.
```

**Long description:**
```
Students learn to read a candle chart, explain buying, selling and short selling, and see for themselves why a pattern in past prices does not tell you the next move. The class votes on every trade in one two-minute game on the projector, then discusses what drove its decisions. Includes a starter, a discussion, three written questions with answers and a five-day decision diary for home.

The game is the Daily Chart on memestreet.net. It uses practice money with no cash value; nothing can be won, lost or paid out, and every company and chart is invented. The class game needs one computer and no accounts. The site has a shop that sells extra practice money and memberships; nothing in the lesson uses it. Not investment advice.
```

**Subjects:** personal finance, economics, business studies, citizenship. **Ages:** 14 to 18.
**Keywords:** stock market, investing, risk and reward, charts, financial literacy, behavioural finance, game.
**Type:** lesson plan. **Price:** free.
