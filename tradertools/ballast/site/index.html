Costs

Your broker's commission is in cTrader's symbol information (or your account's terms). It is charged when a trade opens and again when it closes, and Ballast counts both. The platform's currency-conversion fee is counted by itself.

Check before sending

The loss shown is at the stop, commission included, before slippage. A stop is a price level, not a guaranteed price: in a fast market or over a gap the fill can be worse.

How the size is worked out

Ballast finds the largest size whose loss at your stop, commission included, is no more than the risk you set. It always rounds down to a size the symbol allows, takes every exchange rate on the side that makes the loss larger, and values the loss at the rate it would have when the stop is hit. If even the smallest allowed size would lose more than you set, it says so and places nothing. If your loss limits leave less room than you asked for, the size is cut to fit and the review says so.

Pip: the usual small step of a price (0.0001 on most currency pairs, 0.01 on yen pairs). Lot: the symbol's standard size (100,000 units of a currency pair). R: the money you risk on one trade; a target of 2 R makes twice that if it is reached.

How the limits work

Firms that fund traders close an account that loses more than a set amount in a day or in total, and they count open trades too. Set your firm's numbers here and Ballast does three things: it shows how much room is left, it cuts the size of a new trade so that a stop-out cannot take you past either limit, and it refuses to place orders once a limit is reached. With "count them at their stops", open trades are counted as if every stop were hit, so a new trade can never add up to a breach.

Firms differ: some count the day from the opening balance, some from the higher of balance and equity, some reset at midnight in Prague, others at the broker's server midnight. Read your firm's rules and match them here. The limits protect this panel's orders only: trades placed elsewhere in cTrader are counted, but not blocked.

What this shows

Each open position, what it would lose from its entry if its stop is hit, in your account's money, and the total. A position without a stop has no limit on what it can lose, so it is marked. "If every stop is hit" is your balance afterwards, before commission and slippage.

What R tells you

Each trade placed from Ballast carries the money it risked. A trade that made twice its risk is +2 R; one stopped out is about -1 R. The average R over many trades is your edge per trade: above zero, the way you trade has made money so far; below zero, it has not, whatever the win rate. A position closed in parts counts as one trade.

About the sessions

The conventional hours of the four main trading sessions, each in its own city's time so daylight saving is right everywhere. Prices usually move most when London and New York overlap, and least late in the New York afternoon and around the weekly open. Your broker's own hours for a symbol can differ: cTrader's symbol information has them.
