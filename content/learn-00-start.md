# Start here {start}
How Meme Street works, your first trade with a stop, and where everything is. Read these three first.

## How Meme Street works {welcome} [4]
SUMMARY: Memebucks, fees, what moves prices, and what is the same as a real market and what is not.
Meme Street is a practice stock market. The companies are fictional, the prices are live, and you trade with **memebucks (MB)**: practice money with no cash value that is never paid out. Everyone starts with 1,000 MB. Losing them costs you nothing real, which makes this the cheapest place there is to make a trader's mistakes.

### What you can trade
- **Companies**: about 150 of them in 30 industries, from banks and chip makers to a few imaginary industries (portals, weather control, dreams).
- **Commodities**: crude oil, natural gas, wheat, copper and gold.
- **Indices and funds**: the MSI 50 (the 50 largest companies), an index for each industry, six themed funds, and funds that move two or three times the market, up or down.
- **Options** on every company and commodity.

### What it costs
Every trade costs a fee of **0.1%** of its size, like a broker's commission. Buy 100 MB of a stock and sell it straight back and you have paid about 0.2 MB. There is no spread and no slippage on shares: you always get the price on the screen.

### What moves prices
Prices move with the market as a whole, with each company's news and earnings, with the economy's data releases, and with the way traders as a crowd behave around trends and price levels. **No player's trade ever moves a price**, however big, so nobody can push a stock around: your skill is in reading the market, never in moving it.

HERE: The market drifts slowly upwards over the long run, with corrections, bear markets and long sideways stretches, as real markets do. Charts respect and break support and resistance, form trends and ranges and the classic patterns, at realistic rates. Nothing is rigged for or against you, and no simple rule wins without skill.

### Time runs fast
One **game day** is 30 real minutes: 5 minutes of quiet pre-market, a 20-minute regular session and 5 minutes of quiet after-hours. A game year is about 7.6 real days, so a company reports its earnings roughly every two real days. The market never closes.

TIP: The leaderboards rank your return on the money you put in, so buying more MB never buys you a better place.

Q: You buy 200 MB of a stock and sell it again a moment later at the same price. Roughly what has it cost you?
- Nothing
+ About 0.4 MB in fees
- About 20 MB
WHY: 0.1% on the way in and 0.1% on the way out: 0.2% of 200 MB is 0.4 MB.

WHY: On Meme Street prices follow the market, news and the economy. No trade, however large, moves them.

WHY: Pre-market (5 minutes), the regular session (20) and after-hours (5): a game day every half hour.

## Your first trade, with a stop {first-trade} [5]
SUMMARY: Buying, setting a stop-loss and a take-profit, and sizing the trade from the stop.
A trade has three prices: where you get in, where you admit you were wrong, and where you take the profit. Beginners pick the first and forget the other two. Here is the whole routine, start to finish.

### 1. Pick a stock and read its chart
Open a stock's page from the market table. Look at the chart on a few candle sizes: which way is it going, where has it turned before? The lessons in *Reading charts* cover this.

### 2. Decide where you are wrong before you buy
If you buy because the price bounced off a level, the trade is wrong if the price falls back well below that level. That price is your **stop-loss**. Decide it first, before the price on the screen decides for you.

### 3. Size the trade from the stop
Under the order panel on a stock's page is **Position size and risk**. Type your stop, pick how much of your account you are willing to lose if it is hit (1% is a good start) and a target. It tells you how many MB to buy, what you lose at the stop and what you make at the target, and **Fill in the trade and the bracket** sets it all up for you.

### 4. Buy, then set the bracket
Press Buy, then **Set bracket**: a stop-loss and a take-profit placed together. Whichever is hit first closes the position and cancels the other. You can see and move both on the Holdings page, in the *Take profit* and *Stop-loss* columns, and they show on the chart as red and green lines.

### 5. Leave it alone
The plan was made when you were calm. Moving the stop further away because the price is near it is how small losses become big ones.

HERE: Orders are checked every second and fill at the market price of that moment. If the price jumps straight past your stop you get the new price, as in a real market.

Q: When should you decide where your stop-loss goes?
+ Before you buy
- Once the trade is losing
- After a few days
WHY: The stop is where the trade idea is wrong. Decide it while you are calm, before money is at stake.
Q: You will risk 1% of a 1,000 MB account. The stop is 5% below your entry. How big can the position be?
- 10 MB
+ 200 MB
- 1,000 MB
WHY: 1% of 1,000 MB is 10 MB of risk. A 5% fall on 200 MB is 10 MB, so 200 MB is the most you can buy.
Q: What does a bracket do?
- Buys at two prices
+ Places a stop-loss and a take-profit together; the one that is hit first cancels the other
- Doubles the position
WHY: One-cancels-other: whichever exit is reached first closes the trade and removes the other order.

## Finding your way around {finding-your-way} [4]
SUMMARY: The market, a stock's page, the chart tools, your holdings and portfolio, the calendar and the Daily Chart.
### The market page
Every listing, with its price, the change over the last 30 minutes (one game day) and a small trend line. Search by name, ticker or industry, star stocks to keep a watchlist, and filter by industry. Beside the table: the **news wire** (every headline as it happens), **upcoming events** (earnings and company events with a countdown) and the top of the leaderboard.

### A stock's page
The chart with candle sizes from 30 seconds to a year, **indicators** (17 free, 32 more with Pro), **drawing tools** (lines, channels, Fibonacci, long and short position boxes), the order panel, the position-size tool, the company's description, financials, earnings history and news. The **Options** tab beside the chart has the option chain.

### Your money
- **Holdings**: every open position, its stop-loss and take-profit, and your open orders.
- **Portfolio**: your equity curve, realised and unrealised profit, fees, your best and worst trades.
- **History**: every trade and payment, which you can download.

### The economy
The **Calendar** shows the economic releases (inflation, jobs, growth, interest rates) with their forecasts, and every company's coming earnings report. These move markets; the *Macro* track explains how.

### The Daily Chart
The button in the header opens the **Daily Chart**: one mystery chart a day, the same for everyone, two minutes to trade it. It is the quickest daily practice there is.

TIP: Your chart set-up (indicators, chart type, drawings) is saved to your account, so it follows you to your phone.
